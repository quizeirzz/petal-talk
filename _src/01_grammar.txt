# Grammar
E|Which sentence is grammatically correct?|She goes to school every day.|She go to school every day.|She going to school every day.|She gone to school every day.|The subject "she" is third-person singular, so the simple present verb needs the -s ending: "goes".
E|Choose the correct word: There ___ three apples on the table.|are|is|am|be|"Apples" is plural, so the plural verb "are" is needed.
E|Which is correct?|I have two brothers.|I has two brothers.|I having two brothers.|I haves two brothers.|With "I", the present-tense form of "have" is simply "have".
E|Fill in the blank: My sister ___ a doctor.|is|are|am|be|"My sister" is singular, so we use "is".
E|Which sentence uses the correct form?|They don't like spicy food.|They doesn't like spicy food.|They not like spicy food.|They don't likes spicy food.|With plural subjects like "they", the negative auxiliary is "don't", followed by the base verb "like".
E|Choose the correct question form.|Do you speak English?|Speak you English?|You do speak English?|Does you speak English?|Yes/no questions in the simple present use "Do" plus the base verb for "you".
E|Which sentence is correct?|He can swim very well.|He can swims very well.|He cans swim very well.|He can to swim very well.|After a modal verb such as "can", we use the base form of the verb without "to" or "-s".
E|Choose the correct word: I ___ to the park yesterday.|went|go|goes|going|"Yesterday" signals the past, so the past form "went" is required.
E|Which is the correct plural of "child"?|children|childs|childes|childrens|"Child" has an irregular plural: "children".
E|Fill in the blank: She ___ watching TV right now.|is|are|am|be|"She" takes "is" in the present continuous: "She is watching".
E|Which sentence is correct?|We are students.|We is students.|We am students.|We be students.|The plural subject "we" takes the verb "are".
E|Choose the correct form: How many ___ do you have?|books|book|bookes|book's|"How many" is used with countable plural nouns: "books".
E|Which sentence is correct?|There is a cat under the chair.|There are a cat under the chair.|There be a cat under the chair.|There am a cat under the chair.|A singular noun ("a cat") follows "there is".
E|Choose the correct word: This is ___ pen.|my|me|I|mine|Before a noun we use the possessive adjective "my". "Mine" stands alone without a noun.
E|Which is correct?|I don't have any money.|I don't have no money.|I doesn't have any money.|I don't has any money.|Standard English avoids double negatives; "don't have any" is correct.
E|Fill in the blank: ___ you ready?|Are|Is|Am|Be|"You" always takes "are", even when referring to one person.
E|Which sentence is correct?|She didn't go to the party.|She didn't went to the party.|She don't went to the party.|She didn't goes to the party.|After "didn't", the main verb stays in its base form: "go".
E|Choose the correct sentence.|It is raining today.|It raining today.|It are raining today.|It rain today.|The present continuous needs the auxiliary "is" with "it" plus the -ing verb.
M|Which sentence is correct?|Neither of the answers is correct.|Neither of the answers are correct.|Neither of the answer is correct.|Neither answers is correct.|"Neither" is grammatically singular, so it takes "is". "Of the answers" is just a prepositional phrase.
M|Choose the correct word: She is taller ___ her brother.|than|then|that|as|Comparatives are followed by "than". "Then" refers to time.
M|Which sentence is correct?|I have been living here for five years.|I am living here for five years.|I live here since five years.|I have lived here since five years.|An action that began in the past and continues now uses the present perfect (continuous). "For" goes with a length of time.
M|Fill in the blank: If I ___ you, I would apologize.|were|was|am|be|In hypothetical second conditionals, formal English uses "were" for all subjects.
M|Which sentence is correct?|She suggested that we leave early.|She suggested us to leave early.|She suggested we to leave early.|She suggested leaving us early.|"Suggest" is followed by a that-clause or a gerund, not by an object plus infinitive.
M|Choose the correct form: I look forward to ___ you.|seeing|see|saw|be seeing|In "look forward to", "to" is a preposition, so it is followed by the -ing form.
M|Which sentence is correct?|Each of the students has a book.|Each of the students have a book.|Each of the student has a book.|Each students has a book.|"Each" is singular and takes "has", even when followed by a plural noun phrase.
M|Which sentence is correct?|I wish I had more time.|I wish I have more time.|I wish I will have more time.|I wish I am having more time.|Wishes about the present use the past simple: "I wish I had".
M|Fill in the blank: The news ___ shocking.|was|were|are|have been|"News" is an uncountable noun and takes a singular verb despite ending in -s.
M|Which sentence is correct?|She hardly ever eats meat.|She hardly never eats meat.|She hardly ever eat meat.|She eats hardly never meat.|"Hardly ever" means "almost never"; "hardly never" is a double negative.
M|Choose the correct sentence.|He asked me where I lived.|He asked me where did I live.|He asked me where do I live.|He asked me where I live did.|Indirect questions use statement word order, with no auxiliary "did".
M|Which is correct?|I'd rather stay home tonight.|I'd rather to stay home tonight.|I'd rather staying home tonight.|I'd rather stayed home tonight.|"Would rather" is followed by the base form of the verb.
M|Fill in the blank: She made me ___ for an hour.|wait|to wait|waiting|waited|The causative verb "make" takes an object plus the bare infinitive.
M|Which sentence is correct?|Despite the rain, we went out.|Despite of the rain, we went out.|Despite it rained, we went out.|Despite that rained, we went out.|"Despite" is followed directly by a noun phrase, never by "of".
M|Choose the correct form: It's time we ___ home.|went|go|will go|going|"It's time" plus a subject is followed by the past simple to talk about the present.
M|Which sentence is correct?|The information was very useful.|The informations were very useful.|The information were very useful.|An information was very useful.|"Information" is uncountable, so it has no plural and takes a singular verb.
M|Which is correct?|I enjoy reading in the evening.|I enjoy to read in the evening.|I enjoy read in the evening.|I enjoy for reading in the evening.|"Enjoy" is always followed by a gerund (-ing form).
H|Which sentence is correct?|Had I known, I would have called you.|If I would have known, I would have called you.|Had I knew, I would have called you.|If I had know, I would have called you.|In inverted third conditionals, "Had" moves before the subject and is followed by the past participle "known".
H|Choose the correct sentence.|Not only did she win, but she also set a record.|Not only she won, but she also set a record.|Not only did she won, but she also set a record.|Not only she did win, but also she set a record.|When "not only" begins a sentence, the auxiliary inverts with the subject and the main verb stays in base form.
H|Fill in the blank: The committee ___ divided on the issue.|is|am|be|were being|"Committee" is a collective noun; in standard American usage it takes a singular verb: "is".
H|Which sentence is correct?|Scarcely had we arrived when it started to rain.|Scarcely we had arrived when it started to rain.|Scarcely had we arrived than it started to rain.|Scarcely we arrived when it had started to rain.|"Scarcely ... when" requires inversion after "scarcely" and the past perfect for the earlier action.
H|Which sentence contains a dangling modifier?|Walking to school, the rain started to fall.|Walking to school, I got caught in the rain.|While I was walking to school, it started to rain.|The rain started while I walked to school.|In the first sentence, "walking to school" has no logical subject; the rain was not walking.
H|Choose the correct sentence.|The number of applicants has increased.|The number of applicants have increased.|A number of applicants has increased.|The numbers of applicant has increased.|"The number of" is singular and takes "has". ("A number of" means "many" and takes a plural verb.)
H|Fill in the blank: She insisted that he ___ on time.|be|is|was|will be|After verbs of demand or insistence, the subjunctive base form "be" is used in the that-clause.
H|Which sentence is correct?|Whom did you give the letter to?|Who did you gave the letter to?|Whom gave you the letter?|Who you gave the letter to?|"Whom" is the object of the preposition "to", and the question needs the auxiliary "did" with the base verb "give".
H|Which sentence avoids a misplaced modifier?|She served the children sandwiches on paper plates.|She served sandwiches to the children on paper plates while they sat on them.|She served sandwiches on paper plates to the children who were on them.|On paper plates, the children were served by her sandwiches.|Only the first sentence places the modifier so that the plates clearly hold the sandwiches, not the children.
H|Choose the correct form: No sooner ___ the door than the phone rang.|had I closed|I had closed|did I close|I closed|"No sooner ... than" uses inversion with the past perfect.
X|Which sentence is grammatically correct?|Were she to resign, the project would collapse.|Was she to resign, the project would collapse.|If she were resign, the project would collapse.|Were she resign, the project would collapse.|The formal inverted conditional uses "Were" plus subject plus a "to" infinitive.
X|Choose the sentence with correct parallel structure.|She likes hiking, swimming, and cycling.|She likes hiking, to swim, and cycling.|She likes to hike, swimming, and cycles.|She likes hiking, swim, and to cycle.|Items in a list should share the same grammatical form; all three here are gerunds.
X|Which sentence is correct in formal academic English?|The data suggest a different conclusion.|The data suggests a different conclusions.|The datas suggest a different conclusion.|The data are suggesting different a conclusion.|In formal academic English "data" is treated as plural (the plural of "datum"), so "suggest" is appropriate.
X|Fill in the blank: Many a student ___ struggled with this rule.|has|have|are|were|Despite its plural meaning, "many a" plus a singular noun takes a singular verb.
X|Which sentence is correct?|It is I who am responsible.|It is me who is responsible.|It is I who is responsible.|It is me who am responsible.|In formal English, the verb in the relative clause agrees with the antecedent "I", giving "who am".
X|Which sentence correctly uses the subjunctive?|The teacher recommended that each essay be revised.|The teacher recommended that each essay is revised.|The teacher recommended that each essay was revised.|The teacher recommended that each essay will be revised.|After "recommend that", the subjunctive base form "be" is used, regardless of subject.
