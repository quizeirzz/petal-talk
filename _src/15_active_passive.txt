# Active and Passive Voice
E|Change to passive: "The chef cooks the meal."|The meal is cooked by the chef.|The meal cooked by the chef.|The meal is cooking by the chef.|The chef is cooked by the meal.|Simple present passive = is/are + past participle: "is cooked".
E|Change to passive: "She wrote a letter."|A letter was written by her.|A letter is written by her.|A letter wrote by her.|A letter was wrote by her.|Simple past passive = was/were + past participle: "was written".
E|Which sentence is in the passive voice?|The window was broken by the ball.|The ball broke the window.|The ball is breaking the window.|The ball will break the window.|In the passive, the subject receives the action: "the window was broken".
E|Change to passive: "They clean the classroom every day."|The classroom is cleaned every day.|The classroom cleaned every day.|The classroom is cleaning every day.|The classroom was cleaned every day.|Present simple passive: "is cleaned". The agent "they" can be omitted.
E|Which sentence is in the active voice?|The teacher explained the lesson.|The lesson was explained by the teacher.|The lesson is being explained.|The lesson has been explained.|In the active voice, the subject (the teacher) performs the action.
E|Change to passive: "Tom painted the fence."|The fence was painted by Tom.|The fence is painted by Tom.|The fence painted by Tom.|Tom was painted by the fence.|Past simple passive uses "was painted" and the agent follows "by".
E|Fill in the blank: English ___ all over the world.|is spoken|speaks|is speaking|spoke|The present simple passive "is spoken" shows English receives the action.
E|Change to passive: "The dog chased the cat."|The cat was chased by the dog.|The cat chased by the dog.|The dog was chased by the cat.|The cat is chased by the dog.|The object "the cat" becomes the subject, and "chased" becomes "was chased".
E|Fill in the blank: The letters ___ every morning.|are delivered|is delivered|deliver|delivering|"Letters" is plural, so the passive uses "are delivered".
E|Change to passive: "People speak Spanish in Mexico."|Spanish is spoken in Mexico.|Spanish speaks in Mexico.|Spanish is speaking in Mexico.|Spanish was spoken in Mexico.|The general agent "people" is omitted, and "speak" becomes "is spoken".
E|Which sentence is in the passive voice?|The cake was eaten quickly.|She ate the cake quickly.|She is eating the cake.|She will eat the cake.|"Was eaten" is a passive construction.
E|Change to passive: "The students read the book."|The book was read by the students.|The book is read by the students.|The book reads by the students.|The students were read by the book.|Since "read" here is past, the passive is "was read".
E|Fill in the blank: This house ___ in 1920.|was built|built|is built|was building|A completed past action takes the past simple passive: "was built".
E|Change to passive: "My mother makes the bed."|The bed is made by my mother.|The bed made by my mother.|The bed is making by my mother.|The bed was made by my mother.|Present simple passive: "is made".
E|Which is the correct passive form of "They sell fruit here"?|Fruit is sold here.|Fruit sells here.|Fruit was sold here.|Fruit is selling here.|"Sell" becomes "is sold" in the present simple passive.
E|Fill in the blank: The homework ___ by all the students yesterday.|was finished|finished|is finished|were finished|"Homework" is singular and the action is past, so "was finished" is correct.
E|Change to passive: "The gardener waters the plants."|The plants are watered by the gardener.|The plants is watered by the gardener.|The plants watered by the gardener.|The gardener is watered by the plants.|Plural "plants" takes "are watered".
E|Which sentence is passive?|The song was sung by a famous singer.|A famous singer sang the song.|A famous singer sings songs.|The singer will sing tonight.|"Was sung" is the passive form.
M|Change to passive: "They are building a new bridge."|A new bridge is being built.|A new bridge is built.|A new bridge is building.|A new bridge was being built.|Present continuous passive = is/are + being + past participle.
M|Change to passive: "She has written three novels."|Three novels have been written by her.|Three novels has been written by her.|Three novels were written by her.|Three novels have written by her.|Present perfect passive = have/has + been + past participle; "novels" is plural, so "have".
M|Change to passive: "They will announce the results tomorrow."|The results will be announced tomorrow.|The results will announce tomorrow.|The results are announced tomorrow.|The results will been announced tomorrow.|Future passive = will + be + past participle.
M|Change to passive: "Someone stole my bike."|My bike was stolen.|My bike is stolen.|My bike has stolen.|My bike was stole.|The unknown agent "someone" is omitted; "stole" becomes "was stolen".
M|Change to passive: "The police were questioning the suspect."|The suspect was being questioned by the police.|The suspect was questioned by the police.|The suspect is being questioned by the police.|The suspect were being questioned by the police.|Past continuous passive = was/were + being + past participle; "suspect" is singular.
M|Change to passive: "You must finish the report by Friday."|The report must be finished by Friday.|The report must finished by Friday.|The report must been finished by Friday.|The report is must finished by Friday.|Modal passive = modal + be + past participle.
M|Change to active: "The project was completed by the team."|The team completed the project.|The team completes the project.|The team has completed the project.|The team was completing the project.|The agent "the team" becomes the subject, and the tense (past simple) is kept: "completed".
M|Change to passive: "They had already sent the invitations."|The invitations had already been sent.|The invitations have already been sent.|The invitations were already sent.|The invitations had already sent.|Past perfect passive = had + been + past participle.
M|Change to passive: "Nobody has answered my question."|My question has not been answered.|My question has been not answered.|My question was not answered by nobody.|My question has not answered.|"Nobody" becomes a negative in the passive: "has not been answered".
M|Which sentence is correct?|The thief was caught by the police last night.|The thief was catch by the police last night.|The thief caught by the police last night.|The thief is caught by the police last night.|Past simple passive requires "was" plus the past participle "caught".
M|Change to passive: "She gave me a present."|I was given a present by her.|I was gave a present by her.|A present was given me by her.|Me was given a present by her.|With two objects, the indirect object (me) can become the subject: "I was given".
M|Change to active: "The letter is being typed by the secretary."|The secretary is typing the letter.|The secretary types the letter.|The secretary typed the letter.|The secretary has typed the letter.|The present continuous is kept in the active form: "is typing".
M|Fill in the blank: The new museum ___ next year.|will be opened|will open itself|is open by|will been opened|Future passive: "will be opened".
M|Change to passive: "They should have told us earlier."|We should have been told earlier.|We should be told earlier.|We should have told earlier.|We should been told earlier.|Perfect modal passive = modal + have + been + past participle.
M|Change to passive: "The company is going to launch a new product."|A new product is going to be launched by the company.|A new product is going to launch by the company.|A new product will launched by the company.|A new product is going to launched.|"Be going to" passive = is/are going to + be + past participle.
M|Which sentence is grammatically correct?|The documents have been signed.|The documents have signed by the manager.|The documents has been signed.|The documents been signed.|Present perfect passive with a plural subject: "have been signed".
M|Change to active: "Dinner was being prepared by my father."|My father was preparing dinner.|My father prepared dinner.|My father is preparing dinner.|My father had prepared dinner.|The past continuous tense remains in the active form: "was preparing".
H|Change to passive: "They say that he is a genius."|He is said to be a genius.|He is said that he is a genius.|It is said that he be a genius.|He says to be a genius.|Reporting verbs allow the pattern "subject + is said + to-infinitive".
H|Change to passive: "People believe that the painting was stolen in 1990."|The painting is believed to have been stolen in 1990.|The painting is believed to be stolen in 1990.|The painting was believed stolen in 1990.|The painting believed to have been stolen in 1990.|For an earlier action, use the perfect infinitive: "is believed to have been stolen".
H|Change to passive: "They made him apologize."|He was made to apologize.|He was made apologize.|He was make to apologize.|He made to apologize.|In the passive, "make" is followed by a to-infinitive: "was made to apologize".
H|Change to passive: "We saw her leave the building."|She was seen to leave the building.|She was seen leave the building.|She was saw leaving the building.|She seen to leave the building.|After "see" in the passive, the bare infinitive becomes a to-infinitive.
H|Which sentence is correct?|The patient is being operated on right now.|The patient is being operated right now.|The patient is operating on right now.|The patient is been operated on right now.|With phrasal verbs like "operate on", the preposition stays in the passive.
H|Change to passive: "The teacher is going to explain the rules to us."|The rules are going to be explained to us by the teacher.|We are going to be explained the rules.|The rules are going to explain to us.|The rules going to be explained to us.|"Explain" cannot take a person as its passive subject; the rules are explained to us.
H|Change to passive: "Someone needs to repair this road."|This road needs to be repaired.|This road needs repaired.|This road needs to repair.|This road is needing repair.|"Need" plus passive infinitive: "needs to be repaired" (or "needs repairing").
H|Change to passive: "They won't let us enter."|We won't be allowed to enter.|We won't be let to enter.|We won't let be entered.|We won't be let enter.|"Let" has no natural passive form; "be allowed to" is used instead.
H|Change to passive: "The news surprised everyone."|Everyone was surprised by the news.|Everyone surprised by the news.|Everyone was surprising by the news.|The news was surprised by everyone.|Past simple passive: "was surprised by the news".
H|Change to active: "I was told that the shop had closed."|Someone told me that the shop had closed.|Someone was telling me the shop closed.|Someone tells me that the shop had closed.|I told someone that the shop had closed.|The unknown agent becomes "someone", and the past tense is kept: "told".
X|Change to passive: "It is thought that the bridge collapsed because of the storm."|The bridge is thought to have collapsed because of the storm.|The bridge is thought to collapse because of the storm.|The bridge was thought collapsing because of the storm.|The bridge thought to have collapsed because of the storm.|The perfect infinitive "to have collapsed" shows the earlier action.
X|Which sentence uses the passive correctly in a formal report?|The samples were analyzed, and the results are presented in Table 2.|The samples analyzed, and the results presented in Table 2.|The samples were analyzing, and the results are presenting in Table 2.|The samples have analyzed, and the results presented.|Both clauses require be + past participle: "were analyzed", "are presented".
X|Change to passive: "The storm might have damaged the roof."|The roof might have been damaged by the storm.|The roof might be damaged by the storm.|The roof might have damaged by the storm.|The roof might been damaged by the storm.|Perfect modal passive = might + have + been + past participle.
X|Change to passive: "The manager will have approved the budget by Monday."|The budget will have been approved by Monday.|The budget will be approved by Monday.|The budget will have approved by Monday.|The budget will been approved by Monday.|Future perfect passive = will + have + been + past participle.
X|Which sentence is correct?|Having been warned twice, he finally stopped.|Having warned twice, he finally was stopped.|Being warned twice he finally stopped himself.|Having been warning twice, he finally stopped.|The perfect passive participle "having been warned" shows he received the warnings earlier.
X|Change to passive: "They are said to have hidden the treasure here."|The treasure is said to have been hidden here by them.|The treasure is said to be hidden here.|The treasure said to have been hidden here.|The treasure was said hiding here.|Both the main clause and the infinitive shift to the passive: "is said to have been hidden".
