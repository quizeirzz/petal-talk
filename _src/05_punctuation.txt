# Punctuation
E|Which sentence is punctuated correctly?|I like apples, bananas, and oranges.|I like apples bananas and oranges.|I like, apples, bananas, and oranges.|I like apples, bananas, and, oranges.|Items in a list are separated by commas; the comma before "and" is the optional Oxford comma.
E|Which sentence ends with the correct punctuation?|What time is it?|What time is it.|What time is it,|What time is it;|A direct question ends with a question mark.
E|Choose the correctly punctuated sentence.|Wow, that was amazing!|Wow that was amazing.|Wow, that was amazing,|Wow that, was amazing!|An interjection like "Wow" is followed by a comma, and strong emotion ends with an exclamation mark.
E|Which is correct?|It's a sunny day.|Its a sunny day.|It's a sunny day|Its' a sunny day.|"It's" is the contraction of "it is", and every sentence ends with a punctuation mark.
E|Which sentence uses the apostrophe correctly?|That is Sarah's book.|That is Sarahs book.|That is Sarahs' book.|That is Sarah book's.|A singular possessive is formed by adding an apostrophe + s to the owner's name.
E|Which sentence is correct?|She said, "I am tired."|She said "I am tired".|She said, I am tired.|She said, "I am tired".|A comma introduces the quotation, and the period goes inside the closing quotation mark in American English.
E|Choose the correct punctuation: "Dear Mr. Lee___"|,|.|!|?|In a letter greeting, the name is followed by a comma (or a colon in very formal business letters).
E|Which sentence is correct?|We visited Paris, France, last summer.|We visited Paris France last summer.|We visited, Paris France, last summer.|We visited Paris, France last, summer.|Commas separate the city from the country and close the geographical phrase.
E|Which sentence is correct?|Please sit down.|Please, sit down,|please sit down.|Please sit down|A sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with a period.
E|Choose the correctly punctuated sentence.|Yes, I will come.|Yes I will come,|Yes, I will come|yes, I will come.|Introductory words like "Yes" are followed by a comma, and the sentence ends with a period.
E|Which is correct?|The dogs' bowls are empty.|The dog's bowls are empty for all three dogs.|The dogs bowls' are empty.|The dogs bowl's are empty.|For plural owners ending in -s, the apostrophe goes after the s: "dogs'".
E|Which sentence uses the comma correctly?|After dinner, we went for a walk.|After dinner we went, for a walk.|After, dinner we went for a walk.|After dinner we, went for a walk.|An introductory phrase is followed by a comma.
E|Which sentence is correct?|I can't believe it!|I cant believe it!|I ca'nt believe it!|I can't' believe it!|The apostrophe replaces the missing letters in "cannot": "can't".
E|Choose the correct sentence.|Tom, can you help me?|Tom can you help me?|Tom can, you help me?|Tom, can you, help me?|When you address someone directly, their name is set off with a comma.
E|Which sentence is correct?|My birthday is on March 3, 2010.|My birthday is on March 3 2010.|My birthday is on March, 3, 2010.|My birthday is on March 3. 2010.|In dates, a comma separates the day from the year.
E|Which is correct?|Stop! The light is red.|Stop, The light is red.|Stop The light is red.|Stop; the light is red!|An urgent command ends with an exclamation mark, and the next sentence starts with a capital letter.
E|Choose the correct form.|I'm going to the store.|Im going to the store.|I'am going to the store.|I'm going to the store|"I'm" needs an apostrophe for the missing letter, and the sentence needs a final period.
E|Which sentence is punctuated correctly?|Did you see the movie?|Did you see the movie!|Did you see the movie.|Did you see the movie,|A question ends with a question mark.
M|Which sentence uses the semicolon correctly?|I have a big test tomorrow; I can't go out tonight.|I have a big test tomorrow; because I can't go out tonight.|I have a big test; tomorrow I can't go out tonight.|I have; a big test tomorrow, I can't go out tonight.|A semicolon joins two closely related independent clauses.
M|Which sentence uses the colon correctly?|You need three things: a pen, paper, and patience.|You need: three things, a pen, paper, and patience.|You need three things, a pen: paper and patience.|You need three: things a pen, paper, and patience.|A colon introduces a list after a complete clause.
M|Choose the correctly punctuated sentence.|My brother, who lives in Canada, is visiting.|My brother who lives in Canada, is visiting.|My brother, who lives in Canada is visiting.|My brother who, lives in Canada, is visiting.|A non-restrictive clause is enclosed in a pair of commas.
M|Which sentence is correct?|The children's toys were everywhere.|The childrens' toys were everywhere.|The childrens toys were everywhere.|The children's' toys were everywhere.|"Children" is already plural, so the possessive is formed with apostrophe + s.
M|Which sentence uses the hyphen correctly?|She is a well-known author.|She is a well known-author.|She is a well-known-author.|She-is a well known author.|Compound adjectives before a noun are hyphenated: "well-known author".
M|Choose the correct sentence.|"Where are you going?" she asked.|"Where are you going," she asked?|"Where are you going"? she asked.|"Where are you going?", she asked.|The question mark belongs inside the quotation marks, and no extra comma is needed after it.
M|Which sentence is correct?|However, the results were surprising.|However the results, were surprising.|However; the results were surprising.|However the results were, surprising.|An introductory conjunctive adverb like "However" is followed by a comma.
M|Which sentence correctly uses a dash?|She finally answered - after a long pause - and said yes.|She finally answered after - a long pause and said yes.|She - finally answered after a long pause and said - yes.|She finally answered after a long pause - and said - yes.|Paired dashes set off an interrupting phrase, like parentheses.
M|Which is correct?|Whose coat is this?|Who's coat is this?|Whos coat is this?|Whose' coat is this?|"Whose" shows possession; "who's" means "who is".
M|Choose the correctly punctuated sentence.|The meeting, which lasted two hours, was productive.|The meeting which lasted two hours, was productive.|The meeting, which lasted two hours was productive.|The meeting which, lasted two hours was productive.|Extra, non-essential information is enclosed between two commas.
M|Which sentence is correct?|It was a cold, windy night.|It was a cold windy, night.|It was a cold, windy, night.|It was a cold windy night,|Two coordinate adjectives that could be joined by "and" are separated by a comma.
M|Which sentence is correct?|The students' projects were impressive.|The student's projects were impressive for the whole class.|The students projects' were impressive.|The students's projects were impressive.|For a plural noun ending in -s, add only an apostrophe to show possession.
M|Choose the correct sentence.|I bought milk, eggs, and bread; then I went home.|I bought milk, eggs, and bread, then I went home.|I bought milk eggs and bread; then, I went home.|I bought milk, eggs, and bread then; I went home.|A semicolon correctly separates two independent clauses when the second begins with "then".
M|Which sentence uses parentheses correctly?|The festival (held every July) attracts thousands.|The festival held (every July attracts) thousands.|(The festival held every July) attracts thousands.|The festival held every July attracts (thousands).|Parentheses enclose extra, non-essential information without breaking the main sentence.
M|Which sentence is correct?|She studied French, Spanish, and Italian in college.|She studied French, Spanish, and Italian, in college.|She studied, French, Spanish, and Italian in college.|She studied French Spanish, and Italian in college.|Commas separate list items only; no comma is placed before "in college".
M|Which is correct?|You're going to love this.|Your going to love this.|You're going to love, this.|Youre going to love this.|"You're" is the contraction of "you are"; "your" is possessive.
M|Choose the correct punctuation: "If you finish early___ you can leave."|,|;|:|.|A dependent clause at the beginning of a sentence is followed by a comma.
H|Which sentence uses the Oxford comma?|We invited the teachers, the parents, and the students.|We invited the teachers, the parents and the students.|We invited the teachers, and the parents, and the students.|We invited, the teachers, the parents and the students.|The Oxford (serial) comma is the comma placed before "and" in a list of three or more items.
H|Which sentence is punctuated correctly?|The report is due Friday; however, the data is incomplete.|The report is due Friday, however, the data is incomplete.|The report is due Friday however; the data is incomplete.|The report is due Friday; however the data, is incomplete.|When "however" joins two independent clauses, it follows a semicolon and precedes a comma.
H|Choose the correctly punctuated sentence.|He asked whether we had finished.|He asked whether we had finished?|He asked, whether we had finished?|He asked whether, we had finished.|An indirect question is a statement and ends with a period, not a question mark.
H|Which sentence uses ellipsis correctly?|"I thought you were... never mind," she sighed.|"I thought you were...never mind" she sighed.|"I thought you were..., never mind," she sighed.|"I thought you were .. never mind," she sighed.|An ellipsis (three dots) shows a trailing-off thought; the comma before the closing quotation introduces the speaker tag.
H|Which sentence is correct?|James's car is parked outside.|James' car is parked outside, is the only acceptable form.|Jame's car is parked outside.|James car's is parked outside.|Most style guides form the possessive of a singular name ending in -s by adding apostrophe + s: "James's".
H|Choose the correct sentence.|The recipe calls for the following: flour, sugar, and butter.|The recipe calls for the following; flour, sugar, and butter.|The recipe calls for: the following flour, sugar, and butter.|The recipe calls for the following flour: sugar and butter.|A colon follows a complete clause ("the following") to introduce the list.
H|Which sentence is correct?|Twenty-five students attended the lecture.|Twenty five-students attended the lecture.|Twenty five students, attended the lecture.|Twenty-five-students attended the lecture.|Compound numbers from twenty-one to ninety-nine are hyphenated.
H|Which sentence uses quotation marks correctly?|She called the plan "ambitious," but she supported it.|She called the plan "ambitious", but she supported it.|She called the plan 'ambitious," but she supported it.|She called the plan "ambitious but she supported it."|In American English, commas and periods go inside closing quotation marks.
H|Choose the correctly punctuated sentence.|Although it was late, we kept working; the deadline was close.|Although it was late we kept working, the deadline was close.|Although it was late, we kept working, the deadline was close.|Although it was late; we kept working; the deadline was close.|The introductory clause takes a comma, and the two main clauses are joined by a semicolon.
H|Which sentence is correct?|The self-driving car stopped at the crosswalk.|The self driving car stopped at the crosswalk.|The self-driving-car stopped at the crosswalk.|The self driving-car stopped at the crosswalk.|"Self-" compounds are always hyphenated: "self-driving".
X|Which sentence is punctuated correctly?|My sister-in-law's business, a bakery, opened in May.|My sister-in-laws' business, a bakery opened in May.|My sister in law's business a bakery, opened in May.|My sister-in-law's business a bakery, opened in May.|The possessive goes on the end of the compound noun, and the appositive "a bakery" is enclosed in commas.
X|Choose the correctly punctuated sentence.|The verdict was unanimous: guilty.|The verdict was unanimous; guilty.|The verdict was unanimous, guilty.|The verdict, was unanimous guilty.|A colon can introduce a single word that explains or completes the preceding clause.
X|Which sentence is correct?|"Did she really say 'never'?" he asked.|"Did she really say "never"?" he asked.|"Did she really say 'never?'" he asked.|"Did she really say 'never'"? he asked.|A quotation inside a quotation uses single quotes, and the question mark belongs to the whole question, so it goes after the single quote but inside the double quotes.
X|Choose the sentence with correct punctuation.|The team's goals were clear: finish on time, stay under budget, and satisfy the client.|The team's goals were clear; finish on time, stay under budget, and satisfy the client.|The teams' goals were clear: finish on time stay under budget and satisfy the client.|The team's goals were clear, finish on time; stay under budget; and satisfy the client.|A colon introduces the list, and commas separate its simple items.
X|Which sentence is correct?|The 1990s were a decade of rapid change.|The 1990's were a decade of rapid change.|The 1990s' were a decade of rapid change.|The 1990,s were a decade of rapid change.|Plural decades do not take an apostrophe: "1990s".
X|Which sentence uses punctuation correctly with a list of complex items?|We traveled to Lima, Peru; Quito, Ecuador; and Bogotá, Colombia.|We traveled to Lima, Peru, Quito, Ecuador, and Bogotá, Colombia.|We traveled to Lima; Peru, Quito; Ecuador, and Bogotá; Colombia.|We traveled to Lima, Peru: Quito, Ecuador: and Bogotá, Colombia.|When list items already contain commas, semicolons separate the items.
