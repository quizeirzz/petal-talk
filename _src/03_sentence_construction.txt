# Sentence Construction
E|Which sentence has the correct word order?|I always drink coffee in the morning.|I drink always coffee in the morning.|Always I drink coffee in the morning.|I drink coffee always in the morning.|Adverbs of frequency like "always" go before the main verb.
E|Put the words in the correct order: "a / is / She / teacher"|She is a teacher.|Teacher a is she.|Is she a teacher.|A teacher she is.|The basic English statement order is Subject + Verb + Complement.
E|Which sentence is a complete sentence?|The dog barked loudly.|Barking loudly at night.|Because the dog barked.|The loud dog at night.|A complete sentence needs a subject ("the dog") and a main verb ("barked").
E|Choose the correctly ordered sentence.|We watched a movie last night.|We last night watched a movie.|Last night a movie we watched.|We watched last night a movie.|In English, the object ("a movie") usually comes directly after the verb, with time expressions at the end.
E|Which word order is correct?|She speaks English very well.|She speaks very well English.|She very well speaks English.|Very well she English speaks.|Adverbs of manner usually follow the object: "speaks English very well".
E|Which sentence is correct?|Where do you live?|Where you live?|Where live you?|You live where do?|Wh-questions in the simple present use question word + auxiliary + subject + verb.
E|Fill in the blank with the correct order: "I bought ___."|a beautiful red dress|a red beautiful dress|a dress beautiful red|beautiful a red dress|Opinion adjectives ("beautiful") come before color adjectives ("red").
E|Which is a complete sentence?|Birds fly.|When birds fly.|Birds flying high.|Flying birds in the sky.|"Birds fly" has a subject and a verb and expresses a complete thought.
E|Choose the sentence with correct word order.|He gave her a gift.|He gave a gift she.|He her gave a gift.|Gave he her a gift.|Indirect objects (her) come before direct objects (a gift) when no preposition is used.
E|Which sentence is correct?|I don't know the answer.|I know not the answer.|I don't the answer know.|Not I know the answer.|Negatives in the simple present use "don't" before the main verb.
E|Put the words in order: "the / cat / sleeping / is / sofa / on / The"|The cat is sleeping on the sofa.|The sofa is sleeping on the cat.|On the cat the sofa is sleeping.|The cat on sleeping is the sofa.|Subject + verb + prepositional phrase of place makes the only logical sentence.
E|Which sentence is correct?|There are many stars in the sky.|Many stars there are in the sky.|In the sky many stars there are.|There many stars are in the sky.|"There are" introduces the subject, followed by the place.
E|Choose the correct sentence.|My brother and I went shopping.|Me and my brother went shopping.|My brother and me went shopping.|I and my brother went shopping.|As part of a subject, use "I", and it is polite to mention the other person first.
E|Which sentence is correct?|Can you help me, please?|You can help me please?|Can help you me, please?|Help you can me, please?|Questions with modals begin with the modal verb: "Can you help...?"
E|Which sentence has the correct order?|She usually wakes up at six.|She wakes usually up at six.|Usually wakes she up at six.|She wakes up usually at six.|Frequency adverbs ("usually") go between the subject and the main verb.
E|Which is a complete sentence?|It snowed all night.|Snowing all night long.|Although it snowed all night.|All night the snow.|Only "It snowed all night" has a subject, a finite verb, and a complete idea.
E|Choose the correct sentence.|I like tea, but I don't like coffee.|I like tea, but don't like I coffee.|I like tea but coffee I don't like it.|Tea I like, coffee but not.|Two clauses are joined with "but", and each clause keeps subject-verb order.
E|Which sentence is correct?|What time is it?|What time it is?|Time what is it?|Is what time it?|In questions, the verb "is" comes before the subject "it".
M|Which sentence is a run-on sentence?|I love reading it relaxes me.|I love reading because it relaxes me.|I love reading; it relaxes me.|I love reading. It relaxes me.|Two independent clauses joined with no punctuation or conjunction form a run-on sentence.
M|Which sentence is a fragment?|Although she studied hard for the exam.|She studied hard for the exam.|She studied hard, so she passed.|Studying hard helped her pass.|A clause beginning with "although" cannot stand alone; it needs a main clause.
M|Choose the sentence with correct adjective order.|She has a lovely little old wooden box.|She has a wooden old little lovely box.|She has an old lovely wooden little box.|She has a little wooden lovely old box.|The usual order is opinion, size, age, material: lovely, little, old, wooden.
M|Which sentence correctly combines these ideas: "It was raining. We went for a walk."|Although it was raining, we went for a walk.|It was raining, we went for a walk.|Although it was raining. We went for a walk.|We went for a walk, it was raining.|"Although" turns the first idea into a dependent clause, correctly joined with a comma.
M|Which sentence is correct?|She asked me what my name was.|She asked me what was my name.|She asked me what is your name.|She asked me what my name is it.|Reported questions use statement word order: "what my name was".
M|Choose the sentence that is NOT a comma splice.|The sun set, and the sky turned pink.|The sun set, the sky turned pink.|It was late, we went home.|He was tired, he kept working.|Two independent clauses joined by a comma need a coordinating conjunction such as "and".
M|Which is the best way to combine: "The book was long. The book was interesting."|The book was long but interesting.|The book was long, it was interesting.|The book was long and it interesting.|Long the book was, interesting too.|"But" joins the two contrasting adjectives in one smooth clause.
M|Which sentence uses correct word order with "enough"?|The water is warm enough to swim in.|The water is enough warm to swim in.|The water enough is warm to swim in.|Enough the water is warm to swim in.|"Enough" follows an adjective: "warm enough".
M|Choose the correct sentence.|I have never seen such a beautiful sunset.|I have seen never such a beautiful sunset.|Never I have seen such a beautiful sunset.|I never have seen such beautiful a sunset.|"Never" goes between the auxiliary "have" and the past participle "seen".
M|Which sentence is correct?|She told me that she was tired.|She told to me that she was tired.|She told that she was tired me.|She told me she tired was.|"Tell" takes a direct object (me) without "to", followed by the that-clause.
M|Fill in the blank with the correct structure: "___, the children ran outside."|As soon as the rain stopped|As soon as stopped the rain|The rain as soon as stopped|Stopped the rain as soon as|A time clause starts with the conjunction, followed by subject and verb.
M|Which sentence is correct?|He is too young to drive.|He is too young for drive.|He is young too to drive.|He is too young driving.|"Too + adjective + to-infinitive" is the correct pattern.
M|Choose the sentence with the correct position of "only".|Only she can solve this puzzle.|She can only solve this puzzle, nobody else.|She only can solve this puzzle, nobody else.|She can solve this only puzzle, nobody else.|To mean that nobody else can do it, "only" must go directly before "she".
M|Which sentence is correct?|I explained the problem to her.|I explained her the problem.|I explained to her the problem it.|I her explained the problem.|"Explain" does not take an indirect object without "to": "explain something to someone".
M|Which sentence correctly uses a relative clause?|The woman who lives next door is a nurse.|The woman lives next door who is a nurse.|The woman who she lives next door is a nurse.|Who lives next door the woman is a nurse.|The relative clause "who lives next door" directly follows the noun it describes, with no repeated subject.
M|Choose the correct sentence.|She not only sings but also dances.|She not only sings but dances also too.|Not only she sings but also she dancing.|She sings not only but also dances.|"Not only ... but also" should join two parallel verbs: sings / dances.
M|Which sentence is correct?|I would like to know how much it costs.|I would like to know how much does it cost.|I would like to know how much it cost does.|I would like to know how much costs it.|Embedded questions use statement order: "how much it costs".
H|Which sentence has a misplaced modifier?|She almost drove her kids to school every day.|She drove her kids to school almost every day.|Almost every day, she drove her kids to school.|She drove her kids to school nearly every day.|"Almost drove" suggests she nearly drove but did not; the modifier should be near "every day".
H|Choose the sentence with correct parallel structure.|The job requires patience, creativity, and attention to detail.|The job requires patience, being creative, and to pay attention to detail.|The job requires to be patient, creativity, and attention.|The job requires patience, creative, and detail-oriented.|All items in the list are nouns, which keeps the structure parallel.
H|Which sentence is correct?|Rarely have I seen such dedication.|Rarely I have seen such dedication.|Rarely I seen have such dedication.|Rarely have seen I such dedication.|After a negative adverb like "rarely" at the start, subject and auxiliary invert.
H|Which sentence correctly uses a participle phrase?|Exhausted from the hike, the students fell asleep on the bus.|Exhausted from the hike, the bus carried the sleeping students.|Exhausted from the hike, sleep came to the students on the bus.|Exhausted from the hike, the bus ride made the students sleep.|The phrase must describe the subject that follows it; the students, not the bus, were exhausted.
H|Choose the correctly constructed sentence.|Having finished the report, she emailed it to her manager.|Having finished the report, it was emailed to her manager.|Having finished the report, her manager received it by email.|Having finished the report, the email went to her manager.|The subject after the participle phrase must be the person who finished the report: "she".
H|Which sentence is correct?|The more you practice, the better you become.|The more you practice, you become better.|More you practice, better you become.|The more you practice, the better do you become.|The "the + comparative, the + comparative" structure uses parallel clauses without inversion.
H|Which sentence is correct?|So beautiful was the view that we stayed for hours.|So beautiful the view was that we stayed for hours.|So beautiful was the view we stayed that for hours.|The view so beautiful was that we stayed for hours.|Fronting "so + adjective" causes subject-verb inversion: "was the view".
H|Choose the best sentence combination: "He didn't study. He failed the test."|Because he didn't study, he failed the test.|He didn't study, he failed the test.|He failed the test, because of he didn't study.|He didn't study so that he failed the test.|"Because" correctly shows cause and effect in a well-formed complex sentence.
H|Which sentence is correct?|Little did they know what awaited them.|Little they knew what awaited them.|Little did they knew what awaited them.|They little did know what awaited them.|"Little" as a negative adverb at the start requires inversion: "did they know".
H|Which sentence avoids ambiguity?|While walking the dog, Maria saw a deer.|Walking the dog, a deer was seen by Maria.|A deer was seen walking the dog by Maria.|Maria saw a deer walking the dog.|Only the first sentence makes it clear that Maria, not the deer, was walking the dog.
X|Which sentence uses a cleft structure correctly?|It was the manager who approved the budget.|It was the manager approved the budget.|The manager it was who the budget approved.|It was approved the budget by the manager who.|A cleft sentence uses "It + be + focus + who/that + clause" to emphasize the manager.
X|Choose the sentence with correct structure.|Such was the storm's force that trees were uprooted.|Such the storm's force was that trees were uprooted.|Such was the storm's force trees were uprooted that.|The storm's force was such trees that were uprooted.|"Such + be + noun phrase + that-clause" is a formal inversion pattern for emphasis.
X|Which sentence is correctly constructed?|Whatever happens, we will support you.|Whatever it happens, we will support you.|Whatever happens we will supporting you.|Happens whatever, we will support you.|"Whatever happens" is a complete concessive clause; "whatever" is its subject.
X|Which sentence correctly uses an appositive?|My neighbor, a retired pilot, tells wonderful stories.|My neighbor a retired pilot, tells wonderful stories.|My neighbor, who a retired pilot, tells wonderful stories.|My neighbor, he is a retired pilot, tells wonderful stories.|An appositive noun phrase renames the noun and is set off by commas on both sides.
X|Choose the sentence with correct subordination.|Although the evidence was weak, the jury convicted him.|Although the evidence was weak, but the jury convicted him.|The evidence was weak although, the jury convicted him.|Although the evidence was weak, so the jury convicted him.|Using both "although" and "but/so" creates a double connector; one subordinator is enough.
X|Which sentence is correct?|Only after the meeting did she realize her mistake.|Only after the meeting she realized her mistake.|Only after the meeting she did realize her mistake.|Only after the meeting realized she her mistake.|When "only after" begins a sentence, the main clause requires inversion: "did she realize".
