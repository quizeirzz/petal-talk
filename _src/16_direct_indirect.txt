# Direct and Indirect Speech
E|Change to indirect speech: She said, "I am tired."|She said that she was tired.|She said that I am tired.|She said that she is tired.|She said that I was tired.|In reported speech, "I" becomes "she" and the present "am" shifts to the past "was".
E|Change to indirect speech: He said, "I like pizza."|He said that he liked pizza.|He said that I like pizza.|He said that he likes pizza.|He said that he is liking pizza.|The pronoun changes to "he", and the present simple "like" shifts to "liked".
E|Change to indirect speech: They said, "We are busy."|They said that they were busy.|They said that we are busy.|They said that they are busy.|They said that we were busy.|"We" becomes "they", and "are" shifts back to "were".
E|Change to indirect speech: Tom said, "I will call you."|Tom said that he would call me.|Tom said that he will call you.|Tom said that I would call you.|Tom said that he would call you.|"Will" becomes "would", "I" becomes "he", and "you" (the listener) becomes "me".
E|Which sentence is in direct speech?|Anna said, "I love this song."|Anna said that she loved that song.|Anna said she loved the song.|Anna told me she liked the song.|Direct speech repeats the exact words inside quotation marks.
E|Change to indirect speech: She said, "I can swim."|She said that she could swim.|She said that she can swim.|She said that I could swim.|She said that she can swam.|"Can" changes to "could" in reported speech.
E|Change to indirect speech: He said, "I have a new car."|He said that he had a new car.|He said that he has a new car.|He said that I had a new car.|He said that he have a new car.|The present "have" becomes the past "had".
E|Change to indirect speech: She said, "It is raining."|She said that it was raining.|She said that it is raining.|She said that it rained.|She said that it had raining.|Present continuous "is raining" becomes past continuous "was raining".
E|Change to indirect speech: Mom said, "Dinner is ready."|Mom said that dinner was ready.|Mom said that dinner is ready.|Mom said that dinner has been ready.|Mom said that dinner were ready.|"Is" shifts to "was" after a past reporting verb.
E|Change to indirect speech: He said, "I am studying English."|He said that he was studying English.|He said that he is studying English.|He said that I was studying English.|He said that he studied English.|"Am studying" becomes "was studying", and "I" becomes "he".
E|Change to indirect speech: The teacher said, "You are late."|The teacher said that I was late.|The teacher said that you are late.|The teacher said that you were late to me.|The teacher said that I am late.|The listener "you" becomes "I" (the reporter), and "are" becomes "was".
E|Which reporting verb is correct: She ___ me that she was hungry.|told|said|spoke|talked|"Tell" needs an object ("me"); "say" does not take a person as its object directly.
E|Change to indirect speech: She said, "We live in Rome."|She said that they lived in Rome.|She said that we live in Rome.|She said that they live in Rome.|She said that we lived in Rome.|"We" becomes "they", and "live" becomes "lived".
E|Change to indirect speech: He said, "I don't understand."|He said that he didn't understand.|He said that he doesn't understand.|He said that I didn't understand.|He said that he not understood.|"Don't" becomes "didn't" when the tense shifts back.
E|Change to indirect speech: Lisa said, "My dog is sick."|Lisa said that her dog was sick.|Lisa said that my dog was sick.|Lisa said that her dog is sick.|Lisa said that my dog is sick.|"My" becomes "her", and "is" becomes "was".
E|Which sentence is correct?|He said that he was happy.|He said me that he was happy.|He said to that he was happy.|He said that was he happy.|"Say" is followed directly by "that", not by an object pronoun.
E|Change to indirect speech: She said, "I need help."|She said that she needed help.|She said that she needs help.|She said that I needed help.|She said that she need help.|The verb shifts from present to past: "needed".
E|Change to indirect speech: They said, "We are leaving now."|They said that they were leaving then.|They said that we are leaving now.|They said that they are leaving now.|They said that they were leaving now.|"Now" becomes "then", and the tense shifts back.
M|Change to indirect speech: She said, "I went to the market yesterday."|She said that she had gone to the market the day before.|She said that she went to the market yesterday.|She said that she had gone to the market yesterday.|She said that she has gone to the market the day before.|Past simple shifts to past perfect, and "yesterday" becomes "the day before".
M|Change to indirect speech: He asked, "Where do you live?"|He asked where I lived.|He asked where did I live.|He asked where do you live.|He asked that where I lived.|Reported wh-questions use statement word order, with no auxiliary "do".
M|Change to indirect speech: She asked, "Are you coming?"|She asked if I was coming.|She asked that I was coming.|She asked was I coming.|She asked if are you coming.|Yes/no questions are reported with "if" or "whether" and statement word order.
M|Change to indirect speech: The doctor said, "You must rest."|The doctor said that I had to rest.|The doctor said that I must to rest.|The doctor said that you must rest.|The doctor said that I had rest.|"Must" for obligation usually becomes "had to" in reported speech.
M|Change to indirect speech: He said, "I have finished my work."|He said that he had finished his work.|He said that he has finished his work.|He said that he finished my work.|He said that he had finish his work.|Present perfect becomes past perfect: "had finished".
M|Change to indirect speech: She said to me, "Close the window."|She told me to close the window.|She told me close the window.|She said me to close the window.|She told me that close the window.|Commands are reported with "tell + object + to-infinitive".
M|Change to indirect speech: He said, "Don't touch that."|He told me not to touch that.|He told me to not touching that.|He said me don't touch that.|He told me don't touch that.|Negative commands are reported with "not to + verb".
M|Change to indirect speech: She said, "I will be here tomorrow."|She said that she would be there the next day.|She said that she will be here tomorrow.|She said that she would be here tomorrow.|She said that she would be there tomorrow.|"Will" becomes "would", "here" becomes "there", and "tomorrow" becomes "the next day".
M|Change to indirect speech: He asked, "What time is it?"|He asked what time it was.|He asked what time was it.|He asked what time is it.|He asked that what time it was.|Reported questions use statement order: "what time it was".
M|Change to indirect speech: She said, "I may be late."|She said that she might be late.|She said that she may be late.|She said that she must be late.|She said that she would be late.|"May" changes to "might" in reported speech.
M|Change to indirect speech: They said, "We were watching TV."|They said that they had been watching TV.|They said that they were watching TV.|They said that they watched TV.|They said that they are watching TV.|Past continuous becomes past perfect continuous: "had been watching".
M|Change to indirect speech: He asked me, "Did you see the film?"|He asked me if I had seen the film.|He asked me did I see the film.|He asked me if I saw the film.|He asked me that I had seen the film.|Past simple questions shift to past perfect: "if I had seen".
M|Change to indirect speech: The sign said, "Do not enter."|The sign said not to enter.|The sign said to not entering.|The sign told that do not enter.|The sign said don't enter.|Prohibitions are reported with "not to + verb".
M|Change to indirect speech: She asked, "Can you help me?"|She asked if I could help her.|She asked if I can help her.|She asked could I help her.|She asked that I could help her.|"Can" becomes "could", and the question is reported with "if".
M|Change to indirect speech: He said, "I'm going to buy a car next week."|He said that he was going to buy a car the following week.|He said that he is going to buy a car next week.|He said that he was going to buy a car next week.|He said that he would going to buy a car the next week.|"Am going to" becomes "was going to", and "next week" becomes "the following week".
M|Change to indirect speech: She said, "I saw him this morning."|She said that she had seen him that morning.|She said that she saw him this morning.|She said that she had seen him this morning.|She said that she has seen him that morning.|"Saw" becomes "had seen", and "this morning" becomes "that morning".
M|Change to indirect speech: He said, "Please wait."|He asked me to wait.|He said me to wait.|He asked me that wait.|He told that I wait.|Polite requests with "please" are reported with "ask + object + to-infinitive".
H|Change to indirect speech: She said, "If I had money, I would travel."|She said that if she had money, she would travel.|She said that if she had had money, she would have traveled.|She said that if she has money, she will travel.|She said that if she had money, she will travel.|Second conditionals (hypothetical) usually do not shift further back in reported speech.
H|Change to indirect speech: He said, "The Earth revolves around the Sun."|He said that the Earth revolves around the Sun.|He said that the Earth revolved around the Sun, which is the only correct form.|He said that the Earth had revolved around the Sun.|He said that the Earth would revolve around the Sun.|General truths usually keep the present tense in reported speech.
H|Change to indirect speech: She said, "I wish I were taller."|She said that she wished she were taller.|She said that she wished she had been taller.|She said that she wishes she is taller.|She said that she had wished she were taller.|"Wish + were" is already hypothetical and does not shift further back.
H|Change to indirect speech: He said, "I should have studied harder."|He said that he should have studied harder.|He said that he should had studied harder.|He said that he would have studied harder.|He said that he had should study harder.|Perfect modals like "should have" remain unchanged in reported speech.
H|Change to indirect speech: She said, "Let's go for a walk."|She suggested going for a walk.|She said to go for a walk.|She told us let's go for a walk.|She suggested us to go for a walk.|"Let's" suggestions are reported with "suggest + -ing".
H|Change to indirect speech: He said, "I'll help you if you need me."|He said that he would help me if I needed him.|He said that he will help me if I need him.|He said that he would help me if I would need him.|He said that he would helped me if I needed him.|Both verbs shift back: "will" becomes "would", and "need" becomes "needed".
H|Change to indirect speech: She said, "I had already eaten when he arrived."|She said that she had already eaten when he arrived.|She said that she had had already eaten when he had arrived.|She said that she already ate when he had arrived.|She said that she has already eaten when he arrived.|The past perfect cannot shift further back, so it remains "had already eaten".
H|Change to indirect speech: He asked, "Why didn't you come to the party?"|He asked why I hadn't come to the party.|He asked why didn't I come to the party.|He asked why I didn't come to the party.|He asked that why I hadn't come to the party.|Past simple negative becomes past perfect negative: "hadn't come".
H|Change to indirect speech: She said, "Congratulations on your promotion!"|She congratulated me on my promotion.|She said congratulations to my promotion.|She told me congratulations on your promotion.|She congratulated that I was promoted.|Exclamations of this kind are reported with a specific verb: "congratulate someone on something".
H|Change to indirect speech: The manager said, "We might postpone the meeting."|The manager said that they might postpone the meeting.|The manager said that they may postpone the meeting.|The manager said that we might postponed the meeting.|The manager said that they would postpone the meeting.|"Might" stays "might", and "we" becomes "they".
X|Change to indirect speech: He said, "I would rather you didn't tell anyone."|He said that he would rather I didn't tell anyone.|He said that he would rather I hadn't told anyone.|He said that he would rather you don't tell anyone.|He said that he would rather I wouldn't tell anyone.|"Would rather + past subjunctive" expresses a present preference and does not shift back.
X|Change to indirect speech: She said, "I'll have finished by the time you arrive."|She said that she would have finished by the time I arrived.|She said that she will have finished by the time I arrive.|She said that she would finish by the time I had arrived.|She said that she had finished by the time I arrived.|"Will have finished" becomes "would have finished", and "arrive" becomes "arrived".
X|Change to indirect speech: He said, "You needn't have worried."|He said that I needn't have worried.|He said that I didn't need to worry.|He said that I hadn't needed worrying.|He said that I needn't worried.|"Needn't have + past participle" is a perfect modal and stays unchanged.
X|Which is the correct reported form of: She said, "How beautiful the garden is!"|She exclaimed that the garden was very beautiful.|She asked how beautiful the garden was.|She said that how beautiful the garden is.|She exclaimed how beautiful was the garden.|Exclamations are reported with "exclaimed" and a statement clause using an intensifier such as "very".
X|Change to indirect speech: The judge said, "The defendant must appear in court on Monday."|The judge said that the defendant had to appear in court on the following Monday.|The judge said that the defendant must to appear in court on Monday.|The judge said that the defendant should appeared in court on Monday.|The judge said the defendant must have appeared in court.|"Must" for obligation becomes "had to", and the time reference shifts to "the following Monday".
X|Change to indirect speech: He said, "If only I hadn't sold the house."|He said that if only he hadn't sold the house.|He said that if only he didn't sell the house.|He said that he had regret selling house.|He said if only I hadn't sold the house.|A past perfect regret cannot shift further back; only the pronoun changes.
